1. Find filings with edgar-index, not by crawling EDGAR. Get each
   company's filing history (forms, dates, file numbers) from it in one

2. Fetch each filing once with edgar-fetch, and keep what you fetch

3. Investigate before coding. Before writing the rules for a field, use
   bedrock-ask on a small, varied set of real companies: about three of
   each type (traditional IPO, SPAC, de-SPAC including one with a new

4. Extract in bulk with bedrock-map. Give it a JSON schema per field and
   only the passages it needs. Run --dry-run first and set --max-cost
   from it. Use --escalate sonnet --escalate-when confidence=low. Score
   against the answer key with --gold before every full run. Run the
   full population only when every answer-key item agrees, or each
   disagreement is settled by fixing the rule or correcting the label,
   with the reason in edge_cases.csv. If a field still misses after

5. Test changes on known cases first. Run a new or changed rule on the

6. Send leftovers to the models, not to more code loops. Rows the code

8. Run long jobs with bg-run. Check the AWS sign-in first; if it has

9. If a step takes more than about 10 minutes, find out why before

DEFINITIONS
- Lineage: the chain of reporting entities behind a row's CIK. It
  includes a SPAC the company merged with, the public company it
  reverse-merged into, and the predecessor of a successor issuer, even
  where the merger or succession created a new CIK. "The company" in
  the rules below includes its lineage.
- Effective date: the date the company became subject to SEC reporting
  requirements. By type:
  - Traditional IPO and SPAC IPO: the IPO registration statement's
    effective date (the SEC's EFFECT notice).
  - A company that came out of a de-SPAC: the effective date of the
    original SPAC's IPO.
  - Reverse merger: the effective date of the public company it merged
    into, found by these same rules.
  - Successor issuer (a new CIK that took over a reporting company's
    registration, for example in a holding-company reorganization, a
    reincorporation or an emergence from bankruptcy, usually shown by
    an 8-K12B or 8-K12G3): the predecessor's effective date and type.
  - Direct listing: the effective date of the registration statement it
    listed under.
  - Spin-off and Form 10 (other): the effective date of its Form 10:
    the EFFECT notice if there is one, otherwise 60 days after the Form
    10 was filed (when it takes effect by law).
  - Reg A: the effective date of its Form 8-A.
  - Uplisting: the effective date of the registration that made it
    subject to reporting, not the date it listed on an exchange.
  - Debt-only issuer: the effective date of its debt registration
    statement.
  - Went dark and came back: a company stopped reporting if it filed a
    Form 15 (any variant) or filed no 10-K or 10-Q for more than two
    years. If it later resumed, use the date reporting resumed: the
    effective date of its new registration statement if it filed one,
    otherwise the filing date of its first 10-K or 10-Q after the stop.
- Censoring: if the effective date is before 2015-01-01, record BEFORE
  2015 and don't pin down the exact date. Shortcut: a company that filed
  a 10-K or 10-Q before 2015-01-01 and has not stopped reporting since
  is censored; its date needs no further research. For every other
  company, find the date; if it turns out to be before 2015-01-01,
  record BEFORE 2015.
- Going-public types (column 2). Use the first one that fits:
  1. resumed reporting: went dark and came back, as defined above.
  2. SPAC IPO: the first entity in the lineage to become subject to
     reporting was a SPAC (a blank check company), whether or not it
     has since merged.
  3. reverse merger: a private operating company became public by
     merging into a reporting company that is not a SPAC (often a
     shell), with the private company's owners taking control. Signs:
     an 8-K with Item 2.01 and Form 10 information, or Item 5.06
     (change in shell company status). An ordinary acquisition, where
     the public company's owners keep control, is not one.
  4. spin-off: a parent distributed the company's shares to its own
     shareholders.
  5. traditional IPO: the company's first SEC-registered, underwritten
     sale of its common stock (or units) to the public for cash, with
     the shares listed on a US exchange.
  6. direct listing: listed on a US exchange through a registration
     statement, without an underwritten offering.
  7. uplisting: became subject to reporting when it listed on a US
     exchange, while its shares already traded OTC or abroad. (A
     company that was already reporting before it listed keeps the type
     of its original route.)
  8. Reg A: became subject to reporting after a Regulation A offering.
  9. debt-only: only its debt is registered.
  10. Form 10 (other): registered on Form 10 for any other reason.
  11. other: none of the above (for example, shares first registered on
      an S-4 in a merger). Record why in edge_cases.csv.
- EGC self-report: what the company itself says in a filing. 1 if it
  says it is an emerging growth company, 0 if it says it is not (or is
  no longer), NONE if the filing says nothing either way. Where the
  filing has the EGC checkbox, the checkbox decides (ticked = 1,
  unticked = 0). Otherwise a sentence in the text decides. Registration
  statements, 10-Qs, 10-Ks and 8-Ks filed on or after 2017-04-12 have
  the checkbox; final prospectuses (424B) do not.
- Revenue test: 1 if the company's total revenue for its last full
  fiscal year ended before the date in question is below the EGC limit
  in force on that date, otherwise 0. Total revenue is the total
  revenue line the company reports (for banks: total interest and
  dividend income plus noninterest income). The limit is $1.0 billion
  before 2017-04-12, $1.07 billion from 2017-04-12, and $1.235 billion
  from 2022-09-20.
- Supplemented with the revenue test: use the self-report where it is 1
  or 0. Use the revenue test only where the self-report is NONE. If the
  self-report is NONE and no revenue figure can be found, the answer is
  NONE.

CELL VALUES (both files)
- NONE = truly none (for example, no de-SPAC happened, or no filing in
  the window states EGC status)
- NOT COLLECTED = depends on an effective date before 2015
- EXCLUDED = a row outside the research scope
- Blank = not yet known. None may remain at the end.
- ipo and spac hold 1 or 0. The EGC columns hold 1, 0 or NONE.
- Dates as YYYY-MM-DD. Money in US dollars as plain numbers.
